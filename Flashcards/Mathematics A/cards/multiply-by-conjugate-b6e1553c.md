## Why multiply by a conjugate when simplifying a denominator like $(a+ b)$ with radicals?

---

To eliminate sums of radicals or change the sign in the denominator so it becomes rational (use $(a+b)(a-b)=a^2-b^2$).

Multiplying by the conjugate turns a sum/difference into a difference of squares, removing radicals when they appear as square roots. Example:

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}+\sqrt{q}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{p}-\sqrt{q}}{\sqrt{p}-\sqrt{q}}=\frac{\sqrt{p}-\sqrt{q}}{p-q}.$$
