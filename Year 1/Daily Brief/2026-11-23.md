## 15:00 · Circuits: Three Phase Source

**Yoshi Tsuchiya.** Today's dated deck, YT09, goes from generator windings to phase sequence, balanced phasors and source connections. Equal magnitudes and $120^\circ$ spacing give a balanced set; phase sequence says which phase reaches the same point in its cycle next. In a star connection, one end of each winding is joined at neutral. A phase voltage measures line-to-neutral; a line voltage is the difference between two line potentials.

**Example:** for a balanced star source, take $V_{AN}=230\angle0^\circ$ V and $V_{BN}=230\angle-120^\circ$ V (RMS). Then
$$V_{AB}=V_{AN}-V_{BN}=345+j199.2\approx398.4\angle30^\circ\ \mathrm V.$$
So the line-to-line magnitude is $\sqrt3$ times the line-to-neutral magnitude. Reversing the subscripts reverses the phasor, not just its sign in a table.

**Takeaway and prep:** label the two terminals first, then subtract complex voltages. Sketch the balanced phasor set before using the $\sqrt3$ shortcut. ## 16:00 · Programming

**Teaching team: Steve Gunn and Xu Fang.** A dated topic and lecturer for this slot are not specified. The latest posted deck is Lecture 10, **Timers & Counters**; use it as revision rather than assuming it is today's lecture.

**Example:** a timer fed by a 12 MHz clock through a divide-by-8 prescaler ticks at 1.5 MHz. A compare event every 750 ticks occurs every 500 μs, or 2 kHz. If a pin toggles at each event, one full output period needs two events, so the waveform is 1 kHz.

**Takeaway and prep:** distinguish timer tick rate, event rate and waveform frequency; for a counter that starts at zero, check whether the compare setting itself is included in the count. [Programming module](https://moodle.ecs.soton.ac.uk/course/view.php?id=899) · [Lecture 10 slides](https://moodle.ecs.soton.ac.uk/mod/resource/view.php?id=36219).

## 17:00 · Digital Systems: SystemVerilog, FPGA and D11 Q&A

**Mark Zwolinski.** This is a tutorial in place of the lecture. There is no prepared material. Bring a specific SystemVerilog, FPGA or D11 question rather than expecting a new lecture topic.

**Example to discuss:** for a registered output with active-high reset and enable, sketch three rising clock edges: reset asserted, reset released with enable low, and enable high. The output should reset, hold, then capture the input. A short trace like this makes it easier to pinpoint whether a mismatch concerns reset behaviour, clocking or the next-state logic.

**Takeaway and prep:** bring one small test case with the expected and observed behaviour, or mark one D11 step where the intended behaviour is unclear. [Lecturer announcement](https://moodle.ecs.soton.ac.uk/mod/forum/discuss.php?d=2856).
