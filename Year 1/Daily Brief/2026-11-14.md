### A12 · X14 technical report

**Written deadline: Tuesday 17 November, 16:00.** No submission was recorded on Friday morning; today's status is unconfirmed. For the ELEC rotation, this report concerns **X14 Experimental Design and Practice**.

Use the required structure: **Background, Experimental Setup, Results, Discussion and References**. The most useful weekend milestone is a complete draft with your actual observations and figures in place, leaving time to improve the discussion and references before submission.

Nicolas Green's 9 November clarification is important: A12 assesses technical writing, not whether X14 produced the expected result. An unsuccessful experiment is still reportable. Describe what you did, present what you observed, and distinguish evidence from possible explanations. Do not replace awkward measurements with expected values or invent a successful outcome.

A useful discussion paragraph has three parts: **observation → plausible explanation → a specific check that would distinguish explanations**. For example, an unexpectedly small measured signal could motivate checking the probe configuration or circuit connections; it is not proof that either caused the discrepancy. Use this structure with your own evidence, not as a claim about your experiment.

## A focused maths bridge into next week

**Week-wide preparation, not a class scheduled today.** Bing Chu's published plan moves from first-order ODEs this week to **second-order linear ODEs next week**, with theory and engineering-application videos and Kreyszig, *Advanced Engineering Mathematics*, 10th edition, Chapter 2. The announced ODE assessment window is **23 November, 09:00 to 27 November, 16:00**.

Before moving on, test whether you can choose a method without a section heading telling you which one to use:

1. Differentiate $x\ln x$ and explain why $\int\ln x\,dx=x\ln x-x+C$ for $x>0$. This connects the product rule directly to integration by parts.
2. Solve $y'=3x^2y$ with $y(0)=2$ by separation. The result is $y=2e^{x^3}$; verify it by differentiation.
3. Solve $y'+2y=6$ with $y(0)=1$ using the integrating factor $e^{2x}$. The result is $y=3-2e^{-2x}$; explain why it approaches 3.

These are short diagnostic examples, not extra compulsory assignments. If a step fails, practise that step and retry a similar problem before adding harder algebra. Keep the full first-order tutorial sheet available for exact equations and initial-value/domain questions.
