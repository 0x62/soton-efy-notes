## 09:00 · Circuits: introduction to three-phase circuits

**Lecturer: Yoshishige (Yoshi) Tsuchiya. Confirmed dated topic: YT08.** This is the introduction to three-phase systems, not Monday's YT09 treatment of three-phase sources.

The six-slide deck compares single-, two- and three-phase waveforms, then introduces why three-phase generation, motors and power transmission are useful. Its practical advantages include smoother power delivery, favourable motor operation and economical transmission conductors. The key distinction is between **three supplies with different phases** and simply three identical copies of one supply.

For a balanced three-phase set, the phases are separated by a third of a cycle, or $120^\circ$. A useful illustrative set is

$$v_a=V_m\sin\omega t,\qquad v_b=V_m\sin(\omega t-120^\circ),\qquad v_c=V_m\sin(\omega t+120^\circ).$$

At $\omega t=90^\circ$, these are $V_m$, $-V_m/2$ and $-V_m/2$: their instantaneous sum is zero, although each phase is carrying an alternating signal. This example explains the phase relationship; it is not a claim that today's introductory slides derive every connection or power formula.

**Takeaway:** three-phase systems distribute the waveform around the cycle rather than making all three phases peak together.

**Before class:** sketch three equal-amplitude sinusoids separated by $120^\circ$. Keep amplitude, frequency and phase as separate quantities; detailed source connections come next in the dated sequence.

Sources: [Dated schedule](https://moodle.ecs.soton.ac.uk/mod/page/view.php?id=43581) · [YT08: Introduction to Three Phase](https://moodle.ecs.soton.ac.uk/mod/resource/view.php?id=46246).

## 10:00 · Programming: timers and counters context

**Teaching team: Steve Gunn and Xu Fang; today's individual lecturer and exact Friday agenda are not separately specified.** The latest published lecture is **Lecture 10: Timers & Counters**. The module identifies its slides and recordings as Monday material, so this is relevant current preparation, not a confirmed Friday lesson title.

The deck develops a timer as a **counter plus a clock**. A register holds the running count; clock ticks increment it; overflow or a comparison can trigger an action. Hardware timing continues independently of ordinary instruction execution, unlike a software delay loop.

Separate three quantities:

- **Clock frequency:** the input timing reference.
- **Counter tick frequency:** the clock after any prescaler.
- **Event or waveform frequency:** determined by the number of ticks in a complete event or waveform cycle.

For an illustrative 8-bit counter clocked at $12\,\mathrm{MHz}$ with a divide-by-64 prescaler,

$$f_{\mathrm{tick}}=\frac{12\times10^6}{64}=187{,}500\ \mathrm{Hz},\qquad T_{\mathrm{overflow}}=\frac{256}{187{,}500}=1.3653\ \mathrm{ms}.$$

There are **256** count states from 0 to 255, not 255. A pin toggled once per overflow would need two overflows for a complete square-wave cycle. These are example settings, not prescribed practical register values.

**Takeaway:** count ticks in the whole cycle before converting a register value into a frequency.

**Before class:** be able to distinguish the live counter, its clock/control settings and an output-compare target. Explain why a counter overflow rate and a toggled output frequency differ by a factor of two.

Sources: [Programming module and lecture context](https://moodle.ecs.soton.ac.uk/course/view.php?id=899) · [Lecture 10 slides](https://moodle.ecs.soton.ac.uk/mod/resource/view.php?id=36219).

## 12:00 · Digital Systems: signed numbers

**Lecturer: Geoff Merrett. Confirmed Friday topic: Computer Arithmetic, Signed Numbers.** The dated schedule and slide covers use different part numbers; the titles identify the matching material.

The slides compare sign-and-magnitude, excess/offset notation, one's complement and two's complement. The same bit pattern can have different meanings depending on the representation, so a binary string alone is not enough to identify a signed value.

For **8-bit two's complement**, the most significant bit has weight $-128$ and the other weights are $64,32,16,8,4,2,1$. The range is $-128$ to $+127$. To encode $-13$:

```text
+13:          00001101
invert bits:  11110010
add 1:        11110011
```

Check by decoding: $-128+64+32+16+2+1=-13$. In sign-and-magnitude, by contrast, `10001101` means $-13$; in two's complement that same pattern means $-115$.

**Takeaway:** specify both the number of bits and the representation. Two's complement has one zero and one extra negative value; sign-and-magnitude and one's complement have two representations of zero.

**Before class:** encode and decode a small positive and negative integer in each representation. Check the representable range before assuming a result fits.

Sources: [Digital Systems module and dated teaching schedule](https://moodle.ecs.soton.ac.uk/course/view.php?id=901) · [Signed Numbers slides](https://moodle.ecs.soton.ac.uk/mod/resource/view.php?id=36347).

## 15:00 · Digital Systems: binary arithmetic in hardware

**Lecturer: Geoff Merrett. Confirmed Friday topic: Binary Arithmetic in Hardware.** This follows the signed-number representation lecture.

The material covers half and full adders, multi-bit addition, subtraction using an adder, overflow and arithmetic/status circuitry. A full adder handles two input bits plus a carry-in; chaining the carry produces a multi-bit adder. Subtraction can use $A-B=A+\overline B+1$, with the final carry handled according to the chosen fixed-width interpretation.

A crucial distinction is **carry-out versus signed overflow**. For example, in four-bit arithmetic:

```text
  0111  (+7)
+ 0011  (+3)
------
  1010  (-6 if interpreted as four-bit two's complement)
```

The true answer $+10$ lies outside the signed four-bit range $[-8,7]$. There is signed overflow even though there is no carry out of bit 3. For two's-complement addition, adding two operands with the same sign and obtaining the opposite sign indicates overflow.

**Takeaway:** the hardware bit result is not automatically a valid signed answer. Interpretation and range checks matter alongside the adder itself.

**Before class:** write the full-adder relations $S=A\oplus B\oplus C_{in}$ and $C_{out}=AB+C_{in}(A\oplus B)$, then trace one addition through the carry chain. In the carry expression, $+$ denotes Boolean OR.

Sources: [Digital Systems module](https://moodle.ecs.soton.ac.uk/course/view.php?id=901) · [Binary Arithmetic in Hardware slides](https://moodle.ecs.soton.ac.uk/mod/resource/view.php?id=36348).

## 16:00 · Mathematics: second-order differential equations

**Lecturer: Bing Chu. Confirmed week 9 block: second-order linear ODE theory and engineering applications.** A narrower outline for this particular Friday lecture is not published.

The week's theory material covers distinct, repeated and complex characteristic roots, initial conditions, Euler–Cauchy equations and nonhomogeneous methods. Applications include simple harmonic motion, damping, forcing, resonance and LC/RLC models. The essential starting distinction is **homogeneous versus forced**, followed by the form of the equation's coefficients.

For a homogeneous constant-coefficient example,

$$y''+3y'+2y=0,$$

try $y=e^{rt}$. This gives $r^2+3r+2=(r+1)(r+2)=0$, so

$$y=C_1e^{-t}+C_2e^{-2t}.$$

With $y(0)=1$ and $y'(0)=0$, the two conditions give $C_1+C_2=1$ and $-C_1-2C_2=0$. Thus $C_1=2$, $C_2=-1$, and

$$\boxed{y=2e^{-t}-e^{-2t}}.$$

**Takeaway:** a second-order equation normally needs two independent conditions to determine its two arbitrary constants. Find the general solution first, then apply both conditions.

**Before class:** distinguish the solution forms for two distinct roots, a repeated root and a complex-conjugate pair. A repeated root requires the additional factor $t$; complex roots produce a sine/cosine pair inside an exponential envelope.

Sources: [Week 9 preparation](https://moodle.ecs.soton.ac.uk/mod/page/view.php?id=44582) · [Second-order ODE theory](https://moodle.ecs.soton.ac.uk/mod/resource/view.php?id=42858) · [Engineering applications](https://moodle.ecs.soton.ac.uk/mod/resource/view.php?id=42860).

## 17:00 · Mathematics tutorial: use and check the solution

**Tutorial team; an individual tutor and exact question allocation are not specified.** The second-order ODE block above is the confirmed weekly context, rather than a guarantee of which exercises will be worked today.

A useful tutorial check is to substitute your proposed solution into the equation, not merely check its value at $t=0$. For the lecture example above,

$$y'=-2e^{-t}+2e^{-2t},\qquad y''=2e^{-t}-4e^{-2t}.$$

Then $y''+3y'+2y$ has coefficient $2-6+4=0$ on $e^{-t}$ and $-4+6-2=0$ on $e^{-2t}$. Both the differential equation and the initial conditions are satisfied.

**Takeaway:** initial conditions select a solution; substitution checks whether it solves the equation at all.

**Useful preparation:** take one attempted second-order problem with the characteristic equation, general solution and initial-condition equations written separately. For a forced equation, keep the complementary function and particular integral separate until the final step.

Sources: [Maths preparation and weekly topic](https://moodle.ecs.soton.ac.uk/mod/page/view.php?id=44582) · [ODE theory and examples](https://moodle.ecs.soton.ac.uk/mod/resource/view.php?id=42858).
