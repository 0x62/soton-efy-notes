### Mathematics: prepare before tomorrow’s quiz release

**Limits and Derivatives opens Monday 26 October at 09:00 and is due Friday 30 October at 16:00.** It contains **20 questions and permits one attempt**. The instructions require pen-and-paper computations, specify a **10% penalty per day for late completion**, and require **Finish and submit** when your solutions are final. It is not available to start today.

Use today to practise choosing the appropriate differentiation rule and checking limits before substituting into an indeterminate expression. For example, in the revision exercise

$$\lim_{x\to2}\frac{x^2-4}{x-2},$$

factor the numerator as $(x-2)(x+2)$. For $x\ne2$ the expression equals $x+2$, so the limit is **4**, even though the original expression is undefined at 2. The takeaway is that a limit describes nearby behaviour, not necessarily the value at the point. This is an illustrative revision example, not a quiz question or a claim about the exact assessment questions.

**Circuits week-wide topic: AC power.** The Week 6 slides distinguish instantaneous power, average power, real/reactive power and RMS values. Their complex-power expression uses **peak-amplitude phasors**: $S=\tfrac12 VI^*$. With RMS phasors it becomes $S=V_{\rm rms}I_{\rm rms}^*$. The conjugate and amplitude convention both matter.

For the slides’ in-phase example with peak voltage **8 V** and peak current **4 A**, instantaneous power peaks at **32 W**, but average power is **16 W**. Do not confuse peak instantaneous power with average energy transfer. This is preparation from the week’s slides, not confirmation that Monday covers the entire deck.
