## 09:00 · Optional Programming clinic

No fixed clinic topic or named tutor is listed. The current lab topic is analogue input. A useful check is to distinguish the **ADC clock** (how fast the hardware performs a conversion) from the **sample rate** (how often the program requests a reading). For example, 13 clocks at 187.5 kHz take roughly 69 µs. Asking for one reading every 200 ms still gives just 5 samples per second.

**Takeaway:** make diagnosis concrete by separating the input voltage, the raw count and the calculation that converts the count back to volts. ## 10:00 · Circuits: Three Phase Circuits

**Yoshi Tsuchiya.** YT10 moves from the balanced source to balanced loads, focusing on star connection. In a star load, each impedance is between one line and the common star point. The current in that supply line is the current through its impedance. With three identical impedances, the three current phasors are equal in magnitude and spaced by 120°, so their vector sum is zero. A connected neutral wire therefore carries no current in this balanced case.

**Example:** take a balanced 400 V line-to-line RMS source and a star load with $Z=20\angle 30^\circ\,\Omega$ per phase. Taking phase A's line-to-neutral voltage as the reference, $V_{AN}=400/\sqrt{3}=230.9\angle 0^\circ\,\mathrm V$. Then $I_A=230.9\angle0^\circ/(20\angle30^\circ)=11.55\angle-30^\circ\,\mathrm A$. The other phase currents have the same magnitude and are rotated by $-120^\circ$ and $+120^\circ$; adding the phasors gives zero, not 34.65 A.

**Takeaway:** divide line-to-line voltage by $\sqrt3$ for the balanced star phase-voltage magnitude. Do not divide the star line current by $\sqrt3$. If the impedances differ, test the neutral-current cancellation rather than assuming it.
